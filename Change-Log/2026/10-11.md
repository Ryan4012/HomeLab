# October 11, 2026

## Worked On
- Deploying HashiCorp Vault on its own VM, outside k3s, as the lab's secrets store, then securing access with TLS end to end.

## What I Did
- Provisioned the Vault VM by cloning the existing Debian 13 cloud-init template with Terraform, then installed and configured Vault with an Ansible playbook (Raft storage, systemd service, templated vault.hcl).
- Initialized Vault (5 key shares, 3 needed to unseal), revoked the initial root token, and set up a username/password login.
- Updated the Ansible playbook and vault.hcl template to generate a self-signed cert on the VM (SANs for *.home.arpa and the IP) and enable TLS on the listener.
- Copied the public cert to the Pi's monitoring directory and mounted it into the Caddy container, so Caddy can proxy *.home.arpa to Vault over HTTPS.

## Issues / Findings
- Vault goes on its own VM so cluster nodes can still reach their secrets if k3s is down.
- Putting Caddy in front of Vault only encrypts the browser-to-Caddy hop. Enabling TLS on Vault's own listener is what encrypts the Caddy-to-Vault hop.
- Restarting Vault re-seals it, so every config change or reboot needs a manual unseal. That's the tradeoff of not using auto-unseal.
- The cert is self-signed with an 825-day lifetime. It needs a renewal reminder, and renewal means regenerating on the VM and re-copying the cert to the Pi.
- Once the listener is HTTPS-only, anything still pointing at http://192.168.*.*:8200 breaks.
